# A-90: cold breath scaled by body size (small animals: smaller, shorter, quicker puffs; no trails)

Lane: animal. Branch `fauna/breath-size`, new from origin/main. Queued: start after Zach's RAM install pause.
Read first: COORDINATION.md (rules 15-19), CLAUDE.md, docs/agents/fauna.md, Fauna/CreatureBreathSystem.cs, Ambience/AmbienceSystem.cs
`Breathe()` (~390) and `Puff()` (the player's breath, the reference look).

**Why (Zach, 11-09):** songbirds flying in the cold leave long trails of breath: far too much air for their size. Smaller animals
should breathe less air, more often, in smaller puffs.

**What the code does today:** size = clamp(StandHeight*scale/1.5, 0.3, 1.6), so a songbird gets 30% of a person's puff (floor);
the beat is 3.2 s (1.6 s running) and the exhale window `Exhale` is the same for every size; Breathe() emits at a fixed ~30/s during
the window with a fixed 1.2-1.8 s lifetime and inherits 85% of the animal's velocity, so a fast flier smears a trail.

| Row | Do | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Scale by body size (body mass from StandHeight/BodyLength*scale, one curve, no species literals): puff size and opacity drop steeply for small bodies (lower floor, a songbird about 5-10% of a person); exhale window and lifetime shorter for small; beat faster for small (a songbird about 4x a person's rate, big animals slower than a person); emission count per exhale scales with size, so total vapour ~ body mass. Fliers inherit much less of their velocity (puff stays where it was breathed, then drifts in the wind), so no trail. Very small animals may skip breath entirely below a size. Player breath unchanged. | Logged per-species table (size, beat s, puffs per exhale, puff size, lifetime) for a songbird, a fox, a deer, a horse/large grazer and a colossus-scale body; shots/clip in a cold biome: songbirds flying show at most faint brief dots, no trails; large animals unchanged or larger; particle count in a busy cold scene no higher |
