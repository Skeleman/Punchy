**Why (Zach 11-14):** grass still looks patchy, nowhere continuous. He means the grass itself, not bald regions:
the blades are too sparse and perhaps too small to merge into a field, so you see separate tufts on green ground. He
wants continuous swathes, closer to Breath of the Wild. In the temperate biome, bare grass ground and dirt also read as
recolourings of each other. He wants them to differ in fine texture: dirt speckled and grainy, grass ground textured
with blades. Serves the far view and nature (1) and detail (2).

Today: 28 blades per m² at full density, as tufts of 5 blades on half as many roots (GrassPerSquareMetre, ClumpBlades,
ClumpDensity), 15 vertices per blade, thinning by screen size, and `_PunchyGrassTint` colouring the ground under them.

**Row 2 addition (Zach 11-14):** add a fourth prototype, (d) Ghost-of-Tsushima-style blades, after altunenes' "Cubic
Bezier Grass" (Shadertoy 7fSSRc, MIT: code may be adapted if the copyright line and MIT notice go in the file header).
Take the blade model, not the raymarching: a cubic Bezier spine per blade (base, two control points, tip, with tilt
and bend), width extruded across the spine's tangent with a taper, and the rounded normal (flat normal bent by the
across-blade coordinate). Blades grouped in Voronoi clumps (about 0.2-0.5 m cells, from world position) that share a
facing, height band and colour, so the field reads as patches of growth rather than even stubble. Colour dark at the
root to bright at the tip, tip translucency when backlit. Two vertex budgets by screen size (about 15 near, 7 far) so
the count can rise several times (the reference spaces blades at under a fifth of their height; ours sit about 0.19 m apart; report the ratio). Compare
it like the others, coverage numbers included.
