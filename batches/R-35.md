**Why (Zach 11-14):** he sent a cumulus raymarch shader (MIT, @freeborn) and wants to know which of its features could
improve our clouds' look at little cost, or let us lower our cost. The reference itself is far too slow for us (full
res, 500 steps, all noise computed live). Take ideas, not the whole march. MIT: adapted code keeps the copyright line
and MIT notice in its file header.

Candidates from the reference:
- Look: cauliflower lobes (hemispherical caps on Worley points, 2-3 sizes, caps only grow out of the body, smoother
  broader lower bodies); billowed fine lumps that shade each other; two multiple-scattering octaves (lower extinction,
  flatter phase) lighting the shadow side; dual-lobe phase for a silver lining toward the sun; sky ambient from a
  short upward occlusion test (darker undersides and overhangs); a highlight-compressing curve.
- Cost: hierarchical empty-space skipping (coarse shape distance first, then cheap lobes, full detail only near the
  surface); octave count from pixel footprint with the last octave faded in; first sun step at full detail, longer
  steps coarse with wider density ramps; geometric sun steps (x1.6); early exit at low transmittance.
