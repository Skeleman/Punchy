# R-21: waterfalls fall as sheets, not stepped quads (world lane, #284)

Tree: D:/Unity/Punchy-world (or whichever world tree is free). Branch `world/waterfall-sheets`, new from origin/main.
Read first: COORDINATION.md (rules 11, 15-24), R-9 (river falls that curl and ripple, #253), RiverBaking / RiverData
(fall segments), the river and water shaders, LandmarkEffectSystem (fall mist), W-148 (no water discs, for shared water
code).

**Why (#284, seen in the R-19 review shots, snow peak from 150 m):** a waterfall reads as stacked flat rectangular quads
with hard steps rather than a falling sheet. It needs curvature, broken edges, foam at the steps and soft sides.

| Row | Do | Done when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One sheet.** Fall segments join into one continuous curved sheet (it bows out at the lip and thins as it falls), with no visible steps between quads. Side edges are broken and soft (noise-cut alpha, thin strands peeling off), not straight lines. Same placement, same river data. | Shots, before and after at matching angles: the snow peak fall from 150 m, a river fall from 20 m and from 5 m at its foot |
| 2 | **Foam and detail.** White foam where the sheet hits a ledge or the pool, a mist puff at the foot (reuse LandmarkEffectSystem's mist if it fits), a slightly darker wet rock band beside the fall, and a few spray streaks at the lip (details tenet, kept sparse). LOD by screen size (rule 20): far falls keep the sheet look, near ones add foam and spray. | Shots as row 1; far fall from 400 m still reads as one sheet |
| 3 | **Look fixes (supervisor review 11-13, branch not merged).** (1) The sheet reads as a translucent glass pane: give it an opaque white core. (2) No pane or box outline above the lip or into the sky on stepped chains. (3) No dead-straight bright edge lines. (4) No loose shards at the top. (5) One look per chain: no fall left on the old opaque slab. (6) Foot foam visible at 12 m on pale rock. | The same five views plus one fall on dark rock; pins; sparkleprobe 0; suite |
| 4 | **Fall geometry (Zach 11-13).** "Part of the issue with river geometry is the waterfalls. They should subdivide the mesh for gentler curves and less uniformity. Ideally they would use vertex displacement on the top of the fall to simulate turbulence and again at the base of the fall to ripple in response to the falling cascade". (a) Subdivide the river mesh through each fall: the lip rolls over in a curve instead of a hard crease, the sheet bows and varies in width and thickness along and across, no two falls alike (seeded per fall). (b) Vertex displacement on the water above the lip: the surface speeds up, dips and breaks into turbulence in the last metres before the edge. (c) Vertex displacement at the foot: ripples and a churned mound spreading from the impact line into the pool or the next reach, fading with distance. Subdivision and displacement by screen size (rule 20): a far fall stays one cheap sheet. Mesh shape only: the river course and the fall positions do not move (W-153 owns course fixes). Displacement goes through the shared water path, not a second copy (rule 26); tell the supervisor which shared functions you change. | Before/after at 12 m, 20 m and from the side for three falls (one stepped chain, one single drop, one on dark rock); a 400 m shot still reading as one sheet; a short clip or 6-frame strip showing the lip and foot moving; pins; sparkleprobe 0; suite |
