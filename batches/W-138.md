**Why (Zach 11-10):** today's tunnels bore through small walkable hills (3-14 m), so each arch stands in nearly flat
ground. "Tunnels should go through much larger hills, cliffs, or mountains, and be much longer as a result. It's pretty
senseless to build a tunnel through a tiny walkable hill. I envisioned the tunnels being a means of crossing the mountain
boundaries between biomes (not exclusively, though)."

**Zach 11-11:** "I would allow more flexibility in the incline within a tunnel. Maybe up to 15% slope? I do agree that option C is the best. In terms of gameplay, where exploration is one of the pillars, the sense of discovery of a long tunnel changing biomes would make it exciting. However, I'd like to see more than just.. one tunnel. Any way to make a road take a more meandering path and put it through a mountain or sizeable, but smaller-than-mountain hill? [...] Is there a road that leads to the pier at the entrance to the underwater city at the center map? If not, would there be a good tunnel candidate path?"
So: tunnel grade up to 0.15 (roads outside keep MaxGrade). Option C: one long border-range crossing (range 0 or spine 2), with a waystation or outpost at each end, may run past 500 m if the range is wide. Several tunnels, not one: roads may meander (follow valleys, skirt round, then bore where a ridge, mountain or sizeable hill closes the way), and roads may also go to sights, not only towns: the sea folk landing pier (-1783,-554, no road today), cave mouths, colossi, lookouts.

**Zach 11-11 (later):** "I would like roads to head to more places. The roads also need not travel in an efficient line--there should be breaks an multiple routes. Roads should head to some caves (not all), and all mines. Definitely the seafolk landing--they're integrated with the culture. [...] tone down the height requirement from 40m to 30m."
So the road network grows: every mine gets a road; some cave mouths do (not all: pick by size, nearness to a town, or a sight worth the walk); the sea folk landing pier (-1783,-554) MUST get a road, as part of their culture's ties to the land towns. Roads need not take the cheapest line: allow loops and a second route between some towns (an old road and a new one, a high pass and a valley way), spurs that end at a sight, and breaks (a ford, a washed-out stretch, a ruined bridge, a footpath stretch between two road ends). Keep each break walkable.
